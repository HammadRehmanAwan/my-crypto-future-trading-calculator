Leverage & margin, without the hype

Updated 7 November 2026 · 6 min read

Leverage lets you control a position larger than the money you put up. That deposit is your margin. With 10× leverage, $1,000 of margin controls a $10,000 position. Every 1% move in price is a 10% move in your margin, in either direction.

Leverage doesn't change your profit, it changes your risk

A $10,000 BTC position makes or loses the same dollars whether you opened it with 2× ($5,000 margin) or 20× ($500 margin). What changes is how far price can move before you're liquidated: about 49.5% at 2×, about 4.5% at 20×. High leverage doesn't make a trade more profitable; it makes it more fragile.

Size from risk, not from leverage

Professional traders decide how much they're willing to lose first:

Position size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ Stop-loss distance %

Example: a $5,000 account risking 1% ($50) with a stop 2.5% below entry → position size = $50 ÷ 0.025 = $2,000. Whether you post that with 2× or 10× leverage only changes how much cash sits on the exchange. Make sure liquidation sits well beyond your stop. At 10×, liquidation is about 9.5% away, comfortably past a 2.5% stop.

Rules of thumb

  1. Risk 0.5–2% of your account per trade.
  2. Keep liquidation at least 2× further than your stop-loss.
  3. Treat 20×+ as a tool for very tight, very liquid scalps, not for holding.
  4. Count fees and funding; at high leverage they're a big share of margin.
