Funding rates explained

Updated 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Perpetual futures never expire, so exchanges use funding, a periodic payment between longs and shorts, to keep the contract price close to the spot price.

Who pays whom

  • Positive funding (perp trading above spot): longs pay shorts.
  • Negative funding (perp below spot): shorts pay longs.

The exchange doesn't keep it; it passes between traders. On most major exchanges it's settled every 8 hours (three times a day), though some contracts settle hourly or every 4 hours.

How to calculate your funding cost

Funding per period = Position notional × Funding rate
Cost over a holding period = Notional × Rate × (Hours held ÷ 8)

Funding is charged on the full position size, not your margin. That's why it hurts leveraged traders.

Example

A $10,000 BTC long with $1,000 margin (10×), held for 7 days at a funding rate of +0.01% per 8 h:

$10,000 × 0.0001 × (168 ÷ 8) = $21

That's 2.1% of your margin gone with zero price movement. In hot markets funding can hit 0.05–0.1% per period: the same position would then pay $105–$210 a week, 10–21% of your margin.

What funding tells you

  • Very high positive funding means the market is crowded with leveraged longs. That often precedes sharp "long squeezes" when they get liquidated together.
  • Deeply negative funding means crowded shorts and short-squeeze risk.
  • Funding is a positioning gauge, not a timing signal. Crowded trades can stay crowded for days.

Practical rules

  1. For trades you'll hold more than a day, include funding in your profit target.
  2. Avoid holding high-leverage longs through very high funding unless your edge is large.
  3. On isolated margin, funding paid reduces position margin and moves your liquidation price closer.
