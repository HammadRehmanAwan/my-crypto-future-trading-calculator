How liquidation price is calculated

Updated 7 November 2026 · 6 min read

Your liquidation price is the price at which the exchange force-closes your leveraged position because your margin can no longer cover the loss. Know it before you enter, because once the market gets there, the position is gone.

The formula (isolated margin)

For an isolated-margin perpetual position with leverage L and maintenance-margin rate MMR:

Long: Liquidation = Entry × (1 − 1/L + MMR)
Short: Liquidation = Entry × (1 + 1/L − MMR)

1/L is the share of the position you put up as margin. The MMR (often 0.4–0.5% for BTC and ETH at small sizes) is the cushion the exchange keeps, so you're liquidated before your margin hits zero.

Worked examples

BTC long at $65,000, MMR 0.5%:

Notice the textbook shortcut "100× means a 1% move liquidates you" is wrong in practice. With a 0.5% maintenance margin it's half that. Bitcoin moves 0.5% in minutes on an ordinary day.

Why the real liquidation can be worse

  • Tiered margin: bigger positions get a higher MMR, so they're liquidated sooner.
  • Fees: some exchanges reserve the closing fee from your margin, nudging liquidation closer.
  • Mark price: liquidation triggers on the exchange's mark price (an index of several exchanges), not the last trade, which can differ during spikes.
  • Funding: on isolated margin, funding paid comes out of your position margin over time.
  • Cross margin: with cross margin, your whole account balance backs the position. Liquidation is further away, but you can lose everything in the account.

How to stay away from it

  1. Pick leverage from your stop-loss, not the other way round. If your stop is 4% away, leverage above ~20× puts liquidation before your stop.
  2. Size by risk: decide the dollar amount you're willing to lose, then size the position so your stop-loss loses exactly that.
  3. Always use a stop-loss on the exchange. Alerts can be late; stops execute without you.
  4. Check volatility first: if the coin's normal daily range (ATR) is larger than your distance to liquidation, the trade is a coin flip on noise.
